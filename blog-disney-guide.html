The Ultimate First-Timer's Guide to Walt Walt Disney World® Resort

May 2025 · 8 min read · By Gather & Go Travel Co.

← Back to Tips & Guides

I still remember the first time I took my family to Walt Walt Disney World® Resort. We were so excited — and so completely unprepared. We showed up on a busy Saturday in July, no dining reservations, no real plan, and spent the better part of the afternoon standing in a two-hour line for a ride that took three minutes. By 4pm, everyone was sunburned, hungry, and melting down. It was not the magical experience I had pictured.

That experience is exactly why I became a travel agent specializing in Disney. Because the difference between a frustrating trip and a truly magical one isn't luck — it's planning. And I want every family to have the good kind.

So if this is your first time heading to Walt Walt Disney World® Resort, this guide is for you. I'm going to walk you through everything — the basics, the confusing parts, the things nobody tells you — so you can actually enjoy your trip instead of spending half of it figuring out what to do next.

First Things First: What Even Is Walt Walt Disney World® Resort?

Walt Walt Disney World® Resort is not a theme park. It's a destination. The property covers about 40 square miles — roughly the size of San Francisco — and includes four theme parks, two water parks, more than 30 resort hotels, a shopping and dining district called Disney Springs, a golf course, a sports complex, and more restaurants than most small cities.

The four theme parks are:

  • Magic Kingdom® Park — the classic Disney experience. Cinderella's Castle, Space Mountain, Pirates of the Caribbean, and the nightly fireworks show. This is what most people picture when they think "Disney."
  • EPCOT® — originally built around technology and the future, now also known for its World Showcase (where you can eat and drink your way around 11 countries), festivals, and newer rides like the Guardians of the Galaxy coaster.
  • Disney's Hollywood Studios® — home to Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge, Tower of Terror, Toy Story Land, and the Slinky Dog Dash rollercoaster that kids absolutely lose their minds over.
  • Disney's Animal Kingdom® Theme Park — the largest park by land area. Pandora — The World of Avatar is stunning, and Kilimanjaro Safaris is a genuine highlight for families with younger kids.

Most families plan for 4–7 days, hitting each park at least once and often returning to favorites. One day per park is possible but tight. Two days at Magic Kingdom isn't crazy if you have little ones who want to do it all.

When Should You Go?

Honestly? Avoid summer if you can. July and August are the busiest, hottest, most crowded months at Disney. If your kids are in school, I know it feels like you're stuck going in the summer — but if you can swing a late September or early October trip, or even a weekday trip in January or February, you'll be shocked by the difference. Shorter lines, cooler temperatures, and a noticeably more relaxed experience.

The sweet spot weeks, crowd-wise, tend to be:

  • The week after Labor Day through mid-October (except Columbus Day weekend)
  • The two weeks after Thanksgiving, excluding the holiday itself
  • Mid-January through Presidents' Day weekend
  • Late February through the week before Spring Break

Pro Tip

If you have to go in summer, go on a Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday. Weekends — especially Saturdays — are consistently the busiest days. Even in peak season, a midweek day can feel surprisingly manageable.

Where Should You Stay?

This is one of the most important decisions you'll make, and it's about more than just your room. Staying at a Disney-owned resort hotel unlocks a handful of perks that can genuinely change your trip:

  • Early Park Entry — resort guests get into every theme park 30 minutes before the general public. That sounds small. It is not small. Getting on Tiana's Bayou Adventure or Slinky Dog Dash before the crowds show up is a game changer.
  • Free transportation — buses, monorail, boats, and the Skyliner gondola connect all the parks and resorts. You never need to rent a car or worry about parking.
  • It keeps you in the magic — there's something about staying on Disney property that just feels different. Your kids see Disney buses, Disney characters at the hotel, Disney theming everywhere. The vacation starts the moment you check in.

Disney resorts are broken into three tiers:

Value Resorts

Think Art of Animation, Pop Century, and the All-Star resorts. These are the most budget-friendly on-property option. The rooms are smaller and the theming is big and bold (larger-than-life Disney characters everywhere). Food courts, themed pools, and a great location for the price. Perfect for families who want the perks without the premium.

Moderate Resorts

Port Orleans, Caribbean Beach, and Coronado Springs are the standouts here. You get more space, themed pools with waterslides, and a step up in theming and dining. The sweet spot for a lot of families — you feel like you're staying somewhere special without the full deluxe price tag.

Deluxe Resorts

Grand Floridian, Polynesian, Contemporary, Wilderness Lodge, Beach Club — these are the flagship hotels. Walking or monorail access to Magic Kingdom (at the first three), signature restaurants, beautiful pools, and a level of detail that's genuinely impressive. If you want the full Disney experience and budget isn't the primary concern, a deluxe resort is hard to beat.

Dining Reservations: Do Not Skip This Step

Here's something that catches a lot of first-timers completely off guard: the best Disney restaurants book up weeks, sometimes months, in advance. If you want to have breakfast with Cinderella at her castle, or dinner at Be Our Guest, or any of the character dining experiences — you need a reservation. You cannot just show up and hope for a table.

Disney opens dining reservations 60 days before your check-in date. The most popular restaurants fill up within hours of that window opening — sometimes within minutes. This is one of the biggest advantages of working with a travel agent: we're ready to book the moment that 60-day window opens, so you actually get the table you want.

The Must-Do Character Meals

Cinderella's Royal Table (inside the castle), Topolino's Terrace (characters at breakfast, rooftop views), and the Ohana character breakfast at the Polynesian are consistently the hardest to get and the most memorable. If you have little ones who are into the princesses or classic characters, prioritize these.

That said, not every meal needs to be a sit-down reservation. Disney's quick service restaurants are genuinely good — and part of the experience is grabbing a Dole Whip in Adventureland or a Grey Stuff at Be Our Guest as a walk-up snack. Budget for a mix of both.

Lightning Lane: The Honest Breakdown

This is the part that confuses everyone, so let me just lay it out plainly.

Disney no longer has a free FastPass system. Instead, they have Lightning Lane, which comes in two flavors:

  • Lightning Lane Multi Pass (LLMP) — a paid add-on (around $15–$35 per person per day depending on the date) that lets you book return times for most rides throughout the day. You can only hold one at a time, and you book them through the My Disney Experience app starting at 7am.
  • Lightning Lane Single Pass (LLSP) — a separate paid option for the highest-demand rides (like Tiana's Bayou Adventure or Guardians of the Galaxy). These sell out fast and cost extra on top of LLMP.

Is it worth buying? It depends. On a busy day at Magic Kingdom, yes — absolutely. It can save you hours of waiting. On a slow day in late January, you might not need it at all because the standby lines are already short.

The Best Strategy

If you're buying Lightning Lane Multi Pass, get into the park right at Early Entry and ride two or three headliners in the standby line while crowds are still thin. Then, when you buy LLMP at 7am, focus on using it for the mid-tier attractions and save your stamina for the afternoon.

The Rope Drop Strategy (And Why It Works)

Rope drop is theme park speak for showing up right when the park opens. At Walt Disney World® Resort, if you're staying on property, that means taking advantage of your 30-minute Early Park Entry to be one of the first people through the gates.

Here's the thing about Disney crowds: they are dramatically different before 10am versus after. The first 90 minutes of the day, you can walk onto rides that will have 90-minute waits by lunchtime. It's not an exaggeration. I've walked onto rides with a five-minute wait at 8:30am that had a 75-minute wait by 11am on the same day.

The trade-off is that you have to get up early — sometimes very early. But the payoff is enormous. Families who rope drop consistently get through more of their must-do list with less waiting and less frustration than families who arrive mid-morning.

The Midday Break: Do Not Skip This Either

I cannot tell you how many families I've heard from who say their trip "fell apart" around 2 or 3pm. Kids melting down, parents exhausted, nobody having fun. It's almost always avoidable.

The Florida heat in the middle of the day is no joke, and theme parks are physically exhausting — all that walking, standing, stimulation. If you're staying on property, go back to your hotel in the early afternoon. Let the kids swim or nap. Let the adults rest. Then go back to the parks in the evening when it's cooler, the crowds have thinned slightly, and everyone has their second wind for the fireworks.

The families who build this into their schedule almost universally say it was the best thing they did. The families who power through end up having that miserable afternoon I described — and then they're too tired for the fireworks anyway.

The Disney App: Your Best Friend (And Occasional Enemy)

The My Disney Experience app is essential. You'll use it to check wait times, book Lightning Lane return times, find dining reservations, view the park map, and order mobile food pickup. Download it before you go and get familiar with it.

That said — don't spend your whole trip staring at your phone. It's easy to get sucked into checking wait times every two minutes and optimizing every single decision. The families who seem to enjoy Disney the most are the ones who have a loose plan, use the app when they need it, and otherwise put their phones down and look around.

The castle is beautiful. The fireworks are incredible. Your kids' faces when they see their favorite character are something you don't want to miss because you were checking whether Splash Mountain's wait dropped from 45 to 40 minutes.

What to Pack (The Short Version)

  • Comfortable, broken-in shoes. You will walk 8–12 miles per day. This is not a joke. Wear shoes you've actually worn before, not brand new ones.
  • A poncho for each person. Florida rains every afternoon in summer, and it rains fast. Ponchos from the Disney store are expensive. Buy yours before you go.
  • A portable phone charger. The My Disney Experience app drains your battery. Bring a battery pack.
  • Snacks from outside the park. Disney allows you to bring in food and drinks (no glass, no alcohol). Bringing your own snacks and water can save a significant amount of money over the course of a multi-day trip.
  • Sunscreen. Apply it before you leave the hotel, not when you're already in the Florida sun.
