What to Pack for Walt Disney World® Resort: The Complete Family Checklist

November 2024 · 5 min read · By Gather & Go Travel Co.

← Back to Tips & Guides

I've helped a lot of families plan Walt Disney World® Resort trips, and the packing mistakes I see most often are always the same ones: wrong shoes, no rain gear, dead phone batteries, and spending twice as much on sunscreen inside the park as they would have at a drugstore the day before. These are all preventable.

This is the packing list I give to every family I work with. Save it, print it, or just read through it once before you start throwing things in a suitcase.

The Non-Negotiables

These are the items that will genuinely affect your trip if you don't have them.

👟 Footwear

  • Broken-in walking shoes or sneakers — one pair per person, already worn, not brand new
  • Comfortable sandals for the hotel/resort (optional but nice)
  • Extra socks — you'll want to change them midday if your feet get sweaty
  • Blister bandages — even broken-in shoes can cause blisters after 10 miles of walking

The Shoe Rule

This deserves its own callout: wear shoes you have walked in before. Wearing brand new shoes to Disney is one of the most common mistakes families make. You will walk 8–12 miles per day. New shoes will destroy your feet by noon on Day 1 and potentially ruin the rest of your trip.

☀️ Sun Protection

  • Sunscreen — SPF 50 or higher, water resistant. Buy it before you go; park prices are absurd
  • Sunscreen sticks for kids' faces — easier to apply without fighting them
  • Hats for everyone — Florida sun in summer is intense
  • Sunglasses — especially for kids, who will be looking up at bright attractions all day
  • UV-protective rash guards if visiting water parks or spending time in the pool

🌧️ Rain Gear

  • A poncho per person — Florida rains almost every afternoon in summer, and it rains fast and hard
  • A small dry bag or zip-lock bags to protect phones and valuables on water rides
  • Quick-dry clothing — 100% cotton takes forever to dry in humidity; synthetics are better

Buy Ponchos Before You Go

Disney sells ponchos inside the parks for around $12–$15 each. You can buy perfectly good ones on Amazon for $2–$3. For a family of four, that's a meaningful difference. Pack yours ahead of time.

🔋 Tech & Charging

  • Portable battery pack (power bank) — the My Disney Experience app is battery-intensive
  • A charging cable per device — don't pack just one
  • A small bag or fanny pack to carry your phone, charger, and essentials hands-free
  • Wireless earbuds if you like listening to music or podcasts on long travel days

What to Pack in Your Park Bag

Keep this small and light — you'll be carrying it all day. A small backpack or crossbody bag is ideal.

🎒 Daily Park Bag Essentials

  • Sunscreen (reapply every 2 hours)
  • Portable charger and cable
  • Water bottles — Disney allows you to bring in refillable bottles. Free water cups are available at quick service restaurants if you ask.
  • Snacks — Disney allows outside food and drinks (no alcohol, no glass containers). Snacks from the hotel save money and keep kids from melting down between meals.
  • Ponchos, folded flat
  • A small first aid kit: Band-Aids, blister bandages, children's pain reliever, antacids
  • Wipes — for sticky hands, spills, and general theme park life
  • A compact fan or cooling towel for summer visits
  • Hand sanitizer
  • Autograph book and pen if your kids want character signatures

Clothing Tips

👕 What to Wear

  • Light, moisture-wicking fabrics — cotton feels great at home but holds sweat and stays wet in Florida heat. Synthetics or blends are more comfortable for long park days.
  • Theme your outfits if you want — Disney bounding (dressing inspired by a character without a full costume) is popular and fun. Kids love it.
  • Bring a light layer for evenings and air-conditioned spaces — Florida evenings in winter can be cool, and Disney's indoor attractions are heavily air-conditioned year-round.
  • Swimwear — even if you're not visiting a water park, your hotel pool is a real midday option and a great way for kids to burn energy.
  • Comfortable pajamas — your kids will be exhausted at the end of every day. Make bedtime easy.

Things Most People Forget

🧳 Easy to Overlook

  • Laundry detergent pods if you're doing laundry at the resort — most Disney resorts have laundry facilities
  • A small nightlight for young kids who may be sleeping in an unfamiliar hotel room
  • Ear protection for younger children who are sensitive to loud sounds — some Disney attractions and shows can be very loud
  • Stroller weather shield or cover if you're bringing a stroller
  • Any prescription medications your family uses — enough for the full trip plus extra in case of delays
  • Your My Disney Experience login info saved and accessible — you'll use this app constantly
  • Printed or digital copies of your dining reservations and hotel confirmation

What Not to Bring

Large umbrellas — they're cumbersome and usually not allowed in many areas. Ponchos are a better choice.

Full-size suitcases into the park — there are bag check facilities, but dragging a big bag around all day is miserable. Use a small backpack or crossbody.

Alcohol — Disney doesn't allow you to bring your own alcohol into the parks. It's available for purchase throughout the property (except Magic Kingdom, which is officially dry — though there are a couple of exceptions).

Glass containers — also not allowed in the parks.

New shoes — said it before, worth saying again.
