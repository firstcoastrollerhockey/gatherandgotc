Disney Cruise Line vs. Royal Caribbean: Which Is Right for Your Family?

March 2025 · 7 min read · By Gather & Go Travel Co.

← Back to Tips & Guides

This is the question I get asked more than almost any other. And honestly, it's a great question — because both cruise lines are exceptional for families, but they're trying to do very different things. Picking the wrong one for your family isn't a disaster, but picking the right one can elevate the whole trip from "really fun" to "the best vacation we've ever taken."

I'm going to give you the honest, straightforward comparison — what each line does well, where they fall short, who each one is really built for, and how to decide.

The Philosophy Difference

Before we get into specifics, it helps to understand the fundamental difference in what these two cruise lines are trying to be.

Disney Cruise Line is trying to give you a complete, immersive Disney experience on the water. Everything is intentional, carefully themed, and built around the Disney brand. The entertainment is original Broadway-caliber shows featuring Disney stories. The character interactions feel special and exclusive. The whole ship is designed to feel like an extension of the Disney universe. You pay a premium because you're getting something that only Disney can provide.

Royal Caribbean is trying to give you the most amenity-packed ship possible at a competitive price. They want to wow you with what the ship can do — the surf simulators, the rock climbing walls, the waterslides, the sheer scale of the vessels. The entertainment is good. The kids programs are solid. But the goal is activity and variety, not a specific brand immersion.

Neither approach is wrong. They're just different. Your family's personality will tell you which one fits better.

Character Experiences

This is where Disney wins — and it's not close.

On a Disney cruise, you meet Mickey, Minnie, Goofy, Donald, and princesses in ways that feel genuinely special and intimate. The character meet-and-greets are well-organized, the lines are manageable (especially if you plan ahead), and there are photo opportunities throughout the sailing that you simply cannot replicate on any other cruise line. There's no FastPass or virtual queue — you just show up, wait a reasonable amount of time, and get a real interaction.

Pirate Night — a themed evening where the whole ship transforms, passengers dress as pirates, and there's a deck show complete with fireworks at sea — is a genuine highlight that families talk about for years afterward.

Royal Caribbean has licensed some characters (DreamWorks characters on certain ships, for example), but it's not in the same league. If characters are a significant part of why you're considering a cruise, Disney is the clear answer.

Kids Programs

️ Disney Cruise Line — Oceaneer Club

  • Ages 3–12, themed environments
  • Marvel, Star Wars, and Disney-themed rooms
  • Lab activities, imaginative play, movies
  • It's a Small World Nursery for babies (extra cost)
  • Edge Club for ages 11–14
  • Vibe for teens 14–17

Royal Caribbean — Adventure Ocean

  • Ages 3–17, well-run and engaging
  • Science experiments, art, sports
  • Teen Lounge & dedicated spaces
  • Nursery available on most ships
  • Good for active kids who like variety
  • Less thematic but very solid

Both kids clubs are genuinely good, and most kids love them. Disney's Oceaneer Club has the edge in theming and the immersive environments, but Royal Caribbean's Adventure Ocean works well for kids who are more activity-focused than story-focused. If your kids are passionate about Disney characters and stories, they'll love the Oceaneer Club. If they're more into doing stuff — science experiments, sports, meeting other kids — either will work great.

Onboard Activities & Thrills

This is where Royal Caribbean has a significant advantage, particularly for families with older kids and teens.

Royal Caribbean's larger ships — especially the Icon of the Seas class and the Oasis class — are genuinely staggering in terms of what they have on board. A FlowRider surf simulator. A rock climbing wall. A zip line. Multiple pools and water parks. An ice skating rink. A full Broadway-style theater. Mini golf. Laser tag. The ships are essentially floating resorts, and for families with kids aged 8 and up who want constant stimulation and activity, they're hard to beat.

Disney ships have pools (including an adults-only quiet pool), AquaDuck or AquaMouse water coaster (depending on the ship), and plenty to do — but they're not trying to pack in as many amenities as possible. The experience is more curated and intentional. If "keeping the kids busy with activities all day" is the primary goal, Royal Caribbean wins.

Destinations & Private Islands

Both lines have private island stops, and both are excellent.

Disney's Disney Castaway Cay in the Bahamas is consistently rated as one of the best private island beach days available on any cruise line. It's beautiful, well-organized, and you can walk straight off the ship onto the island (no tendering). There's a family beach, an adults-only beach, bike rentals, snorkeling, water sports, and a Disney-themed atmosphere throughout. It's a genuinely spectacular day.

Royal Caribbean's Perfect Day at CocoCay in the Bahamas has gone through an enormous transformation in recent years and is now exceptional — waterpark, beach, lagoon, zipline, and even a hot air balloon. For thrill-seeking families, it might actually edge out Disney Castaway Cay. The waterpark is enormous and impressive.

On Itineraries

Royal Caribbean offers significantly more itinerary variety — Alaska, Norway, the Mediterranean, Australia, Japan, and more. Disney Cruise Line is primarily Caribbean, Bahamas, and Mediterranean (seasonally). If a specific destination is driving your decision, this matters.

Entertainment

Disney's onboard entertainment is some of the best at sea, full stop. The shows are original productions — think Frozen, Disney Dreams, The Little Mermaid — performed in a proper theater with impressive production values. They're not watered-down versions of Broadway shows; they're created specifically for Disney Cruise Line and they're genuinely impressive.

Royal Caribbean's entertainment is also strong — they've had full Broadway shows like Grease, Mamma Mia, and others on their ships. The quality has improved significantly in recent years. But the Disney shows have a cohesiveness and emotional pull that's hard to replicate when you have the full Disney catalog to work with.

Price

Disney Cruise Line is more expensive. Sometimes significantly more expensive. For a comparable itinerary, you're often looking at 30–50% more than Royal Caribbean. Whether that premium is worth it comes down to how much the Disney brand experience matters to your family.

If your kids are Disney-obsessed and the characters, the shows, and the immersive environment are a big draw — yes, the premium is often worth it. If your family just wants a great cruise and doesn't have strong Disney attachment, Royal Caribbean gives you more ship and more activities for less money.

The Bottom Line: Who Should Choose Which?

Choose Disney Cruise Line if: your kids are passionate about Disney, characters matter, the Pirate Night concept excites you, you want the most curated and intentional family experience, or you're already planning a Walt Disney World® Resort trip and want to combine the two.

Choose Royal Caribbean if: you have active older kids who want nonstop activities, budget is a meaningful factor, you're interested in destinations Disney doesn't sail to, or your family isn't particularly tied to the Disney brand and just wants a great cruise with tons to do.

My Honest Take

For families with kids under 10 who love Disney, Disney Cruise Line is usually the right answer — the character interactions and the Pirate Night alone make it special. For families with older kids or teens, or for families where the Disney premium is hard to justify, Royal Caribbean delivers tremendous value and an incredible experience. Both are among the best in family cruising. You genuinely can't go wrong — it just depends on what matters most to your family.
